# Sydney Property Developer Economics

*Revenue, costs, cashflow, profit and entry capital for small, medium and large developers, plus luxury projects on the North Shore and in the Eastern Suburbs.*

*Compiled November 2026. Project-level figures marked "illustrative" are estimates built from the sources listed at the end; they are not data from real projects. This is general information, not financial advice.*

---

## 1. Revenue and expenditure by developer size

There is no public dataset splitting Sydney developers' finances by size. Most developers are private and publish nothing, so this combines NSW Government cost modelling, finance-market pricing and the published accounts of large developers.

### Baseline: cost to deliver one Sydney apartment (2025)

From the CIE modelling for NSW, for one apartment in a seven-storey infill building, averaged across Sydney:

| Component | Cost per dwelling | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Construction | $417,000 | ~40% |
| Required developer margin | $175,000 | ~17% |
| Financing | $119,000 | ~11% |
| Land acquisition | $113,000 | ~11% |
| Other (fees, site works, sales, government charges) | ~$225,000 | ~21% |
| **Total** | **~$1,049,000** | |

- Total cost per dwelling rose from about $917,000 (2023) to about $1,049,000 (2025), up 14%, mainly driven by construction (+18%).
- Average sale price was about $1,103,000, so projects only just clear their required margin.
- Infrastructure contributions were 2.9% of development costs, or 4.4% including land tax and stamp duty. Industry groups that also count GST and other taxes report much higher totals.

### How the breakdown changes with size

| | Small (duplexes, townhouses, boutique blocks; usually under ~$10m cost) | Medium (mid-rise, ~30–200 units) | Large (Mirvac, Meriton, Lendlease, Stockland etc.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue sources** | Sales of finished dwellings; sometimes selling a DA-approved site or keeping units to rent | Off-the-plan presales and completed-stock sales; some JV and build-to-rent sell-downs | Residential sales plus commercial development, rent, funds management fees, hotels |
| **Finance cost** | Highest: mostly private lenders | High: non-bank, or bank with strong presales | Lowest: corporate debt and capital partners |
| **Construction** | Pays external builder's margin; no buying scale | Mostly external builder | Often in-house, keeping the builder's margin |
| **Land** | Bought at market, often through agents | Market purchases plus options/JVs | Land banks, options, capital-efficient deals |
| **Indicative target margin** | 15–25% on cost | 18–25% on cost | ~18–24% gross margin |

**Finance is the biggest cost difference between tiers.**

- Many major banks won't consider projects under about $10m total cost, so small developers mostly use private credit.
- Indicative private rates: 8.5–11.5% (institutional non-bank senior), 9.5–13% (mid-market non-bank), 10.75–14% (short-term private), 14–22% (mezzanine), plus 1–3% establishment fees.
- Private lenders typically want 20–35% equity and an 18–25% project margin on cost.
- Mirvac guided to a weighted average cost of debt of about 5.4% for FY26, roughly half what small developers pay.

**Sales costs also benefit from scale.** Meriton reportedly saved around 2% of revenue by selling directly to overseas buyers without agents.

### Large developers' accounts

**Mirvac (FY26, year to June 2026):**
- Revenue of about $2.62bn and statutory net profit of $677m.
- Operating profit of $508m (up 7%), from 2,130 residential settlements.
- Development EBIT of $270m (up 52%).
- Residential gross margin of 23.9% excluding impaired projects, or 20.1% including them, within its 18–22% target range.
- Much of the revenue comes from a ~$10.5bn rental portfolio and funds management, which smooths earnings.

**Meriton (private):** 2025 revenue of about $2.42bn, from development and investment, building management, property finance, residential and serviced apartments, and fixed income.

### Market conditions (late 2026)

- The RBA raised rates on 3 February, 17 March and 5 May 2026, lifting private-credit costs.
- Western Sydney developer Bathla entered voluntary administration in August 2026 with about $3.6bn in private-credit debt and 2,000 homes mid-construction.
- About 75% of apartments approved in metropolitan Sydney since 2020 haven't started construction.
- Apartment development is generally feasible only east of Parramatta.

## 2. Cashflow and profit

### The core pattern

1. **Equity goes out first:** deposit, land, stamp duty, DA and consultants.
2. **Construction is debt-funded,** with interest usually capitalised.
3. **Revenue arrives at the end,** when buyers settle. NSW off-the-plan deposits are generally held in trust, so they mostly don't fund construction.

A project can be profitable on paper and still fail if costs or delays exhaust cash before settlement.

### Small developer — illustrative 6-townhouse project (~2.5 years)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue (net of GST and agent fees) | ~$8.7m |
| Land plus stamp duty | ~$2.6m |
| Construction | ~$3.6m |
| Fees, approvals, contributions, land tax | ~$0.6m |
| Finance (private, ~11–13% + 2% fee) | ~$0.6m |
| **Total cost** | **~$7.4m** |
| **Pre-tax profit** | **~$1.3m (~18% on cost)** |

- Equity of ~$2.2m tied up for ~2.5 years; peak debt ~$5m.
- Return on equity ~60% over the project, roughly 20%+ a year pre-tax.
- A 10% construction overrun costs ~$360k; a 6-month delay costs ~$300k. Personal guarantees are standard.

### Medium developer — illustrative 100-unit mid-rise (~4.5 years)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Revenue (net of GST and selling costs) | ~$110m |
| Land plus duty | ~$12m |
| Construction | ~$48m |
| Fees, contributions, taxes, marketing | ~$17m |
| Finance (~9–12%) | ~$15m |
| **Total cost** | **~$92m** |
| **Pre-tax profit** | **~$18m (~20% on cost)** |

- Equity of ~$20–25m locked up for 4–5 years; ~13–16% a year once time is counted.
- Bank funding usually depends on hitting presale targets first.
- A 5% price fall removes ~$5.5m of profit; a 6-month delay adds ~$3–4m interest.

### Large developer

- Recurring rental and fee income covers overheads and interest every year.
- Settlements spread across many projects; corporate debt around 5–6%.
- Mirvac FY26 net operating cash flow was about $875m, with distributions of about $376m.
- Margins are similar in percentage terms; the advantage is volume, diversification and cost of capital.

### Summary comparison

| | Small | Medium | Large |
|---|---|---|---|
| Margin on cost | 15–25% | 18–25% | ~18–24% gross |
| Return on equity per year | ~15–25% (volatile) | ~12–18% | ~6–10% company-wide, steady |
| Cash timing | One lump at the end | Lumpy, project-by-project | Continuous |
| Cost of debt | 10–14%+ | 9–12% | ~5–6% |
| Main profit risks | Overruns, delay, failed sales | Presale shortfall, rate rises, price falls | Impairments, cycle-wide downturn |

## 4. Luxury projects: North Shore and Eastern Suburbs

### What makes luxury different

**Very high sale prices per square metre:**
- Bondi Junction sub-penthouse: $11m, over $55,000/sqm.
- Potts Point: Victoria Street ~$70,000/sqm; Macleay Street/Darlinghurst Road $62,000–80,000/sqm.
- ELIZA Darling Point: ~$75,000/sqm average.
- Beachfront Bondi: over $100,000/sqm.
- Wirra, Neutral Bay: $4.45–15m for 140–210 sqm (roughly $30,000–70,000/sqm).

**Construction dearer but not proportionally:** custom/architectural homes ~$3,000 to over $8,000/sqm — about 1.5–2.5× mainstream, while prices are 2–5× higher.

**Land is the biggest cost:** typically 25–35% of total cost (vs ~11% for mainstream mid-rise). Example: an approved Bronte Road site (455 sqm, 7 luxury apartments plus commercial) sold for about $7.5m.

**Different buyers:** wealthy downsizers often paying cash, less exposed to rates, but few in number. New luxury apartment prices in Sydney rose about 34% since 2020.

### Luxury by developer size

| | Small (spec homes, 3–8 units) | Medium/boutique specialists (10–60 units) | Large developers entering luxury |
|---|---|---|---|
| Examples | Private builder-developers | Central Element, Stargate, Abadeen, Podia, Iris Capital | Lendlease One Darling Point (41 units, from ~$6.5m) |
| Equity needed | ~$3–6m | ~$10–40m | Balance sheet |
| Typical profit | ~$0.5m/yr | ~$2–8m/yr | Small part of group earnings |
| Margin on cost | 10–20% | 18–30% | ~20–25% |

### Luxury-specific risks

- **Price sensitivity:** a 10% price fall cuts the apartment example's profit by more than half.
- **Slow sales of top units:** penthouses (e.g. the ~$30m Billyard Ave penthouse) can be a large share of revenue and sit unsold.
- **Planning:** Woollahra, Waverley and Mosman tightly control height and density.
- **Upside:** cash buyers cushion rate rises; record sales can lift prices across a building.

## 5. Entry range to buy and develop luxury apartments and homes

Assumes construction of $5,000–9,000/sqm, equity of 30–40% of total cost, and current NSW stamp duty (premium rate above ~$3.7m). All figures are estimates.

| Project | Land | Total cost | **Equity needed** | Likely sale value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Luxury home (knockdown-rebuild, 350–550 sqm) | $4–8m | $7.5–16m | **$2.5–6m** | $9–20m |
| Luxury duplex (two ~300 sqm homes) | $3.5–6m | $8–12m | **$2.5–4.5m** | $10–16m |
| Boutique apartments, 4–7 units | $5–10m | $16–28m | **$5–10m** | $22–40m |
| Boutique apartments, 10–20 units | $10–25m | $35–80m | **$11–28m** | $45–110m |
| Larger luxury block, 20–50 units | $25–60m+ | $80–250m | **$25–80m** | $100–320m |

### What moves the range

- **Location:** North Shore (Cremorne, Northbridge, Neutral Bay, inner Mosman) at the lower end; prime Eastern Suburbs and waterfront at the upper end or above. Waterfront sites alone can exceed $20–30m.
- **Specification:** basement, pool, lift and premium finishes can push construction to $9,000+/sqm.
- **Track record:** first-timers need 35–40% equity at private rates; experienced sponsors 25–30%.

### Cash needed beyond equity

- Contingency buffer: ~10–15% of equity.
- Pre-DA costs: $150k–500k (home) or $0.5–2m (apartments), before any loan.
- Holding costs: $0.3–1m+ if luxury stock sells slowly.

### Realistic minimum entry

- **~$2.5–3m cash** for a lower-range North Shore knockdown-rebuild or duplex.
- **~$5–6m+** for a first boutique apartment project.
- Projects above ~$30m total cost are hard to finance without a track record.

### Ways to reduce equity

- Joint venture with a landowner (site in exchange for units or profit share).
- Option the site, win the DA, then develop with partners or sell the approved site.
- Syndicated, mezzanine or preferred equity (costs 14–20%+).
- Build for a client instead of on spec.
