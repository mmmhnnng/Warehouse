# Property development in Sydney: chat notes

*Conversation exported 23 November 2026. All figures are illustrative ballparks, not financial advice. Get a quantity surveyor, accountant and finance broker to test any real project.*

---

## 1. What are the broad property categories for development?

Property development is usually grouped into a handful of broad categories, each with its own economics, approval pathways and risk profile.

- **Residential** is the largest category: detached houses, duplexes and townhouses, apartments and other multi-unit housing, plus land subdivision. Build-to-rent, student accommodation, retirement living and affordable or social housing are often treated as niches of their own.
- **Commercial** covers office buildings, from small suburban offices to CBD towers, plus medical and professional suites.
- **Retail** ranges from shopfronts and strip shops to neighbourhood centres, large-format retail and shopping centres.
- **Industrial** includes warehouses, logistics and distribution centres, manufacturing plants, light industrial units, data centres and cold storage.
- **Hospitality and leisure** covers hotels, serviced apartments, resorts, entertainment venues and tourism facilities.
- **Mixed-use** combines two or more of the above, typically retail at ground level with offices or apartments above.
- **Special purpose or social infrastructure** includes childcare centres, schools, hospitals, aged care, places of worship and community facilities.
- **Rural and agricultural** covers farmland, rural-residential lifestyle lots and agribusiness facilities.

Projects are also described by site type: **greenfield** (undeveloped land), **brownfield** (previously used, sometimes contaminated) and **infill or redevelopment** (sites within existing urban areas).

What you can build depends on zoning. In NSW, Local Environmental Plans use zones such as R2 Low Density Residential, E2 Commercial Centre, E4 General Industrial and MU1 Mixed Use.

## 2. Residential categories

From lowest to highest density:

- **Land subdivision**: splitting a parcel into lots, as Torrens title (separate freehold) or community/strata title (shared common property).
- **Detached houses**: from custom builds and knockdown-rebuilds to volume project homes on greenfield estates.
- **Secondary dwellings (granny flats)**: a small second dwelling on the same lot, usually not separately titled. Often approved through faster complying development in NSW.
- **Dual occupancy**: two dwellings on one lot, attached (duplex) or detached, often subdivided afterwards.
- **Townhouses and terraces**: attached dwellings, usually two storeys, each with its own ground-floor entry. Larger projects are called multi-dwelling housing.
- **Manor houses and small low-rise apartments**: three to four dwellings in a two-storey building designed to look like a large house. A "missing middle" form encouraged by recent NSW reforms.
- **Residential flat buildings (apartments)**: walk-ups through to high-rise, almost always strata titled, with extra design standards in NSW for three storeys and above.
- **Shop-top housing**: apartments above ground-floor retail or commercial space.
- **Specialised residential**: build-to-rent, seniors housing, student accommodation, co-living, boarding houses, and affordable or social housing.
- **Rural-residential and lifestyle lots**: large lots (roughly 1–40 hectares) on the urban fringe.

NSW residential zones: R1 General Residential, R2 Low Density, R3 Medium Density, R4 High Density and R5 Large Lot Residential. Check the current LEP and the NSW Planning Portal for any specific site.

## 3. What it takes to reach $10M, $50M and $100M net worth

### The core math

- **Developer margin**: lenders typically want 15–20% profit on total development cost.
- **Return on equity**: banks fund roughly 60–80% of costs, so leverage magnifies returns on your equity, in both directions.
- **Tax**: profit from developing to sell is ordinary income (company rate 25–30%), not a discounted capital gain.

Compounding at 20% a year after tax turns $1M into about $10M in 12–13 years, $50M in about 21 years, and $100M in about 25 years.

### Option 1: Townhouses, duplexes and single dwellings

- **Typical duplex**: ~$3.8M total cost, profit $400–800k, equity $0.8–1.2M, 2–2.5 years.
- **Typical 6-unit townhouse project**: ~$9M total cost, profit $1.5–2M, equity $2–3M, 2.5–3 years.
- **$10M**: ~20 duplexes or ~8 small townhouse projects, 8–13 years. Very achievable.
- **$50M**: ~100 duplex-equivalents, 8–10 sites running at once, usually with your own construction company. 18–22 years.
- **$100M**: hits the ceiling; requires volume estates, land subdivision or moving into apartments.

### Option 2: Residential apartment buildings

- **Typical 40-unit mid-rise**: ~$40M total cost, ~$48M sales, ~$8M profit, $8–12M equity, 3.5–5 years. Presales usually required.
- **Joint ventures**: investors fund most equity; the developer earns a 2–4% fee plus 30–50% of profit above investors' preferred return.
- **$10M**: 2–3 projects, 8–12 years including a track record.
- **$50M**: 8–12 mid-rise or a couple of 150–300-unit projects, 15–20 years, team of 15–40.
- **$100M**: $500M–$1B+ of projects in progress, institutional partners, 20+ years.
- **Risks**: builder insolvency, cost blowouts, presale defaults, valuation drops before settlement, interest rates, approval times, stricter NSW compliance.

### Option 3: Luxury apartments and homes

- **Typical luxury home**: ~$13.5M cost, ~$17M sale, ~$3.5M profit, ~$6M equity, 6–18 months to sell.
- **Typical boutique luxury apartments (15 units)**: ~$58M cost, ~$75M sales, ~$15M profit; harder presales, often private credit at 10–14%.
- **$10M**: 2–4 luxury homes from $3–6M starting capital, 6–10 years.
- **$50M**: a handful of boutique projects, 12–20 years.
- **$100M**: possible with few projects, but highly concentrated.

### Side by side

| | Small-scale residential | Apartments | Luxury |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profit per project | $0.4–2M | $3–10M+ | $3–15M+ |
| Equity per project | $1–3M | $8–15M (or a JV) | $6–20M |
| Project duration | 2–3 yrs | 3.5–5 yrs | 2–4 yrs |
| $10M | 8–13 yrs, very achievable | 8–12 yrs | 6–10 yrs if well capitalised |
| $50M | 18–22 yrs, needs own build arm | 15–20 yrs | 12–20 yrs |
| $100M | Rarely without changing strategy | 20+ yrs, institutional scale | Possible, very concentrated |
| Main risk | Margin squeeze | Leverage and builder failure | Market cycle and few buyers |

### Beyond the math

- Most developers graduate: duplexes → townhouses → mid-rise apartments.
- Many hold some completed stock or build-to-rent for income, growth and deferred tax.
- Surviving downturns matters more than any single deal.
- The team (broker, QS, planner, builder, agent) becomes the business.

## 4. Easiest strategy to reach $50M

**Mid-rise apartments, funded mostly through joint ventures, after a short apprenticeship in townhouses.**

- **Versus small-scale**: the problem is scale. One 40–80-unit project can make as much as 10–20 duplexes for similar effort.
- **Versus luxury**: luxury relies on large, concentrated bets in the most volatile segment. Apartments sell to a broad pool, presales prove demand before construction, and risk spreads across projects.
- **The JV model**: investors provide most equity; your fee and profit share let net worth grow faster than your own capital.

**Realistic sequence**

1. 3–5 years of duplexes or townhouses to build capital, track record and relationships.
2. A first small apartment project (15–30 units) with a JV partner.
3. Scale to 40–100-unit projects, two or three at once.
4. Hold some stock or move into build-to-rent.

Roughly 15–20 years to $50M. Common derailers: over-leverage into a downturn, builder failure mid-construction, and buying sites that only work if prices keep rising.

## 5. The luxury market: margins, volatility and risk

### Margins

- Percentage targets (20–30% on cost) aren't far above mainstream (15–20%); the difference is dollar scale.
- **Extra margin comes from** scarce land, design and product quality, and few direct comparisons.
- **Margin leaks through** much higher build costs per square metre, heavy land tax and expensive debt during slow sales, prestige selling costs, and over-capitalising.

### Volatility

- Prestige prices rise faster in booms and fall harder in downturns; at the top end there may simply be no buyers.
- Drivers: wealth effects (shares, business sales, bonuses), international and expat buyers, confidence, and policy (stamp duty, land tax, foreign buyer rules).
- Few sales make prices hard to read, which affects both lending and buyer confidence.

### Risk

- **Liquidity**: a finished luxury home can sit unsold for 12–24 months.
- **Concentration**: one project can tie up as much capital as five to ten mainstream ones.
- **Finance**: lower bank lending, harder presales, expensive private credit.
- **Construction**: few capable builders, specialist trades, demanding buyers and costly defect disputes.
- **Planning**: heritage controls, view protection and well-resourced objectors.
- **Taste**: designs can date during a three-year project.

### How experienced luxury developers manage it

Low leverage and deep reserves, buying land well, refined rather than idiosyncratic design, strong prestige agent and buyer networks, and willingness to build only once a buyer has signed, or to live in or rent the property.

## 6. Getting started: suburbs, home types, prices and margins

### Market and planning context (late 2026)

- Sydney values in August 2026 were about 7.1% below their February 2026 peak, and all four major banks expected further falls over the year (OpenAgent). Sites are cheaper, but price sales conservatively.
- Since 1 July 2024, dual occupancies and semi-detached dwellings are permitted with consent in all R2 zones across NSW. Stage 2 of the Low and Mid-Rise Housing Policy (from 28 February 2025) allows terraces, townhouses, low and mid-rise apartments and shop-top housing within 800 metres of identified town centres and stations.

### Suburb tiers for a first duplex (built to sell)

| Tier | Example suburbs | Median house | Duplex site | Sale per side | Profit | Margin on cost | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer west and south-west (thin for sale) | Penrith, St Marys, Campbelltown, Minto, Liverpool | $0.85–1.0M | $0.9–1.2M | $0.9–1.15M | $100–300k | 5–12% | $0.5–0.7M |
| **Middle-ring west and south (best starter)** | Merrylands, Granville, Chester Hill, Bankstown, Padstow, Revesby | $1.3–1.6M | $1.3–1.7M | $1.6–1.9M | $300–550k | 10–18% | $0.8–1.1M |
| North-west, Ryde, Sutherland (step two) | Carlingford, Epping, Ryde, Winston Hills, Caringbah | $1.6–2.5M | $1.8–2.5M | $2.1–2.8M | $0.5–1.0M | 12–20% | $1.1–1.6M |
| Prestige (not a starter) | Eastern suburbs, lower north shore, northern beaches | $3–6M+ | $3.5–6M+ | $3.5–6M | $0.8–2M | 10–25% | $2.5–5M |

Margins are after GST and all costs, before income tax. Supporting medians: Penrith houses ~$850–950k (HtAG); Liverpool, Campbelltown, Macquarie Fields and Minto under $900k; Merrylands $1.45M and Padstow $1.606M (PRD via Canstar); Winston Hills $1.64M.

Why the outer west is harder: duplex build costs are similar across Sydney, typically $350–600k per dwelling or $700k–1.2M for the pair, at about $2,500–4,500 per square metre. Where new homes sell for around $1M, the gap between cost and sale price is too small.

### Worked example: middle-ring duplex

Assumptions: $1.45M site, two dwellings of ~190 sqm built for $1M combined, each side selling for $1.85M, about two years.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale proceeds (2 sides) | $3,700k |
| Land | –$1,450k |
| Stamp duty and acquisition | –$70k |
| Design and approvals | –$60k |
| Demolition and site works | –$70k |
| Build | –$1,000k |
| Council contributions and utilities | –$50k |
| Finance and holding | –$250k |
| Selling (2.5%) | –$93k |
| GST (margin scheme) | –$205k |
| **Profit** | **~$453k (≈14% on cost)** |
| Rough equity needed | ~$0.9M |

### Sensitivity

| Scenario | Approx. profit |
|---|---|
| Base case | $453k |
| Sale price –5% | ~$290k |
| Sale price –10% | ~$125k |
| Build cost +10% | ~$350k |
| Sale –5% and build +10% | ~$185k |
| 6-month delay | ~$390k |

Key lessons: GST under the margin scheme is a large cost first-timers often miss; sale price is the biggest driver; each month of delay costs about $10k.

### Which product type to start with

| Product | Your equity | Timeline | Margin on cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Granny flat behind an existing house | $50–100k | 4–8 months | n/a (kept as a rental) | Cheapest way to learn approvals and building; ~$160–210k for a 60 sqm 2-bed |
| Knockdown-rebuild single house | $0.6–1.0M | 18–24 months | 8–15% | Simple, but only one home to sell |
| Duplex (dual occupancy) | $0.6–1.6M | 20–30 months | 10–18% | Best first development |
| 3–6 townhouses, terraces or a manor house | $1.5–3M | 30–36 months | 15–20% | Second step, in Stage 2 areas near stations; per-sqm costs fall with 4+ dwellings |

### How to get started

1. **Choose two or three middle-ring council areas** and learn their rules. Minimum duplex lot sizes vary (e.g. Fairfield: 600 sqm near services and transport, 900 sqm elsewhere in R2).
2. **Run your own feasibility on 20–30 listed sites** before making offers, pricing sales from recent new-duplex sales nearby.
3. **Get a town planner to check a site before buying**: lot width, slope, easements, flooding, bushfire, heritage. Pre-DA meetings with council are usually free.
4. **Get development finance pre-approved** and keep a 10–15% cash buffer.
5. **Have a fallback**: be able to hold one or both dwellings as rentals if the market falls.
